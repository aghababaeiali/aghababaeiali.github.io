01 / About

From Slurs dataset
to generative AI

My NLP journey started with my thesis, working alongside Prof. Tomaso Erseghe at the University of Padova. We were training BERT and RoBERTa on the Slurs dataset, real accounts of verbal harassment labeled by social psychologists, and the question that grabbed me was: which words are actually driving the model's decisions?

That question pulled me deep into explainability tools, Integrated Gradients, Captum, SpaCy, and the broader Hugging Face ecosystem. My progress on the thesis caught my supervisor's attention, and he invited me to join his wider research team, a collaboration spanning the University of Padova and SWPS University in Warsaw, bringing together NLP engineers and social psychologists. That work led to a published arXiv paper with me as first co-author.

Since then I have been building production-shaped LLM systems end to end: agent architecture, evaluation, cloud infrastructure, and CI/CD. That includes a multi-agent dealership assistant where authorization is enforced structurally by PostgreSQL row-level security rather than by prompting, a property-insights agent that routes between SQL, RAG, and a cancellation-risk model on Azure, and a LangGraph research agent that searches arXiv live and self-evaluates with RAGAS (Faithfulness 0.921 across 20 queries). I moved to Rotterdam in 2026 and I am looking for AI and LLM engineering roles in the Netherlands where the work is serious and the systems actually ship.

Agentic AI · Security · AWS 2026

Dealership Agent

A customer-service agent for a used-car dealership, built to answer a harder question than "can it find a car": can the LLM ever be tricked into reading another customer's data? A LangGraph supervisor routes each message to one or two permission-scoped sub-agents behind an MCP tool server; identity is authenticated once at the edge and never enters a tool schema or prompt, so PostgreSQL row-level security is what actually rejects a cross-customer query, not an application-level filter or a system-prompt instruction.

Built a 71-case labelled eval set for hallucinated action claims (replies falsely confirming a cancellation or handoff that no tool result backs); a two-stage verifier raised accuracy from 0.690 to 0.901. A follow-up refinement that looked plausible measured worse on the same eval set and was reverted, documented rather than hidden. Deployed to ECS Fargate, RDS/pgvector, and an ALB via Terraform and OIDC-secured CI/CD; live verification against the running service surfaced two production-only bugs invisible to local testing.

Agentic AI · RAG · Azure 2026

Property Insights Assistant

Natural-language questions over a portfolio of short-stay property listings and bookings, with a model surfacing which listings are likely to cancel. An 8-node LangGraph workflow routes each question to a SQL node, a RAG node over listing descriptions, or a risk node, then runs every answer through deterministic grounding, a sampled LLM-as-judge check, and a human-review queue that feeds a regression suite.

Removed target leakage from the cancellation-risk model, compared four model families via cross-validation, and reported an honest 0.706 ROC-AUC rather than a leaked, inflated one. Migrated the stack end-to-end to Azure (OpenAI, AI Search, ML) with Terraform and OIDC-secured CI/CD; Application Insights tracing shows p50 29ms on the risk endpoint.

Agentic AI · LangGraph · Evaluation 2026

arXiv Search Assistant

An agentic AI system built with LangGraph that answers research questions by searching arXiv live, retrieving relevant paper chunks, detecting contradictions between sources, and generating grounded answers with inline citations. The agent reasons and loops: if initial search results are insufficient, it reformulates queries and retries up to 3 times before gracefully exiting. Evaluated with RAGAS across 20 queries: Faithfulness 0.921, Answer Relevancy 0.885.

Key engineering decisions: per-paper ChromaDB retrieval with global re-ranking to prevent volume bias, session-isolated vector collections via UUID namespacing, dynamic query contextualization to bridge the conversational-to-academic embedding gap, and a gated RAGAS evaluator that runs offline to keep response time acceptable in the live demo.

Explainability · Research 2025

Explainable NLP with Integrated Gradients

Fine-tuned BERT, RoBERTa, and BERTAgent on the Slurs dataset, women's accounts of verbal harassment labeled for shame, fear, and sexual objectification. Applied Integrated Gradients via Captum for word-level attribution, benchmarked against Sequential IG, DeepLIFT, and GradientSHAP using comprehensiveness, sufficiency, and approximation error. Developed a custom rendering pipeline with SpaCy to handle negations and subword tokens. Validated findings with social psychologists, and applied the same method to a small-dataset scenario using deliberate overfitting to surface class-defining keywords. First co-author, arXiv preprint (2025).
